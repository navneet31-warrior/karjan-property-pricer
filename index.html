Who is the customer for each unit

Minimum cash and income for the most expensive version of each unit (top floor, or high end for shops), for a buyer with no other EMIs, using the typical loan terms under Taxes & lending. "Can buy" and "Cannot buy" test the nine buyer templates as listed, including their own EMIs, loan rates and tenures.

Shop price by carpet size

Who can buy each unit

Tested at the most expensive price of each unit (top floor for flats, high end for shops). A buyer qualifies when savings cover the down payment plus GST, stamp duty and registration, and income supports the loan within their FOIR. The test never changes a price; it tells you which sizes your buyers can reach.

How it works

Market rate. Median carpet-area rate of switched-on Karjan RERA comparables (one value per project), moved by your positioning.

Profit-led rate. Fixed cost = land + land duty + construction + approvals + architect fees + GujRERA fee + contingency. Revenue = rate × (flat carpet × average floor rise + Σ shop super area × shop multiple). Rate = fixed cost ÷ (1 − sales & finance % − required margin) ÷ that area; with a ₹ amount, (fixed cost + amount) ÷ (1 − sales & finance %) ÷ area.

Flat prices. Price = rate × carpet area × (1 + floor rise × floor). Price rises with size, so a 3 BHK always costs more than a smaller 2 BHK.

Shop prices by size. Shop ₹/sq ft super = Karjan residential rate S1 × (your rate ÷ comparable median) × ratio × (super area ÷ 300)elasticity, less the upper-floor discount where it applies. Ratio, elasticity and discount come from a regression of 48 listings S29–S34 against locality residential rates S1 S35 S36. Revenue counts the low ratio.

Viability. Net margin = (revenue − fixed cost − sales & finance) ÷ revenue, compared with the 5–15% earned by small developers S14.

Buyer check. Maximum price = the smaller of (loan capacity + savings) ÷ (1 + taxes) and savings ÷ (1 − loan-to-value + taxes), solved inside each RBI loan slab and GST band. Largest affordable size = the size whose top price that many buyers can still pay.

Academic estimation model built for a case study. Listing prices are asking prices, only three Karjan projects pass screening by default, and the shop curve explains about a third of the variation between listings (R² ≈ 0.32).
